# Car Hire Fuel Policy Explained: Camel Global's Guide for UK Travellers Hiring a Car in Spain

A car hire fuel policy sets out how you receive the car's fuel tank and how you're expected to return it, and it has a direct effect on what you end up paying. In Spain, most hire companies use either full-to-full (you get it full, you return it full) or full-to-empty (you prepay for a full tank and hand it back on empty). Camel Global's meet and greet partners use a simpler approach: you leave a refundable fuel deposit and get back everything except the fuel you actually used, so you're never paying for someone else's petrol.

## What Exactly Is a Car Hire Fuel Policy?

A fuel policy is the rule a hire company applies to the fuel level in the car at pickup and drop-off. It's written into every hire agreement, and it's one of the biggest hidden causes of extra cost when British travellers hire cars abroad. In Spain, the three policies you'll typically come across are:

- **Full-to-full**: the car is delivered with a full tank and you return it full. Fair in theory, but you often end up buying a splash of extra fuel at an airport filling station just to be safe, and no station will refund you for slightly overfilling.
- **Full-to-empty**: you prepay for a full tank upfront, drive as much or as little as you like, and return the car on any fuel level, including empty. This sounds convenient but almost always means paying for fuel you never burned, since few holidaymakers manage to use an entire tank.
- **Deposit-based, pay-for-what-you-use**: you leave a refundable deposit that covers a full tank, and after the hire the company works out how much fuel you actually used and refunds the difference. This is the model used across Camel Global's network of local partners.

## Full-to-Full vs Full-to-Empty: Which Costs More?

Here's how the two traditional policies compare for a typical week-long trip to Spain, alongside the deposit-based model.

| Fuel policy | How it works | Where you can lose money |
|---|---|---|
| Full-to-full | Collect full, return full | Topping up at an expensive airport station before drop-off, or being charged for a tank that isn't exactly full |
| Full-to-empty | Pay for a full tank upfront | Paying for fuel you never use if you don't drain the tank |
| Deposit-based (Camel Global partners) | Leave a refundable deposit, only pay for fuel used | Nothing extra, as long as you know how much fuel you actually used |

The deposit-based approach removes the guesswork. You're not racing to find a petrol station near Málaga or Alicante airport with one eye on the departures board, and you're not handing over money for a full tank you'll return three-quarters full.

## How Do I Avoid Hidden Car Hire Fees in Spain?

Hidden fees usually creep in through fuel charges, surprise excess deductions, or add-ons you didn't ask for. A few practical steps:

- Confirm the fuel arrangement before you book, not after you collect the car.
- Check what's included in the price shown, rather than assuming extras like a second driver or child seat are free.
- Ask for a proper invoice for your hire, issued by the operator supplying the car, so you have a clear record of what you paid for.
- Book through a platform where you can see and compare offers upfront, rather than negotiating at a counter under time pressure.

Camel Global's model is built around this transparency: you see the offer, you pay the hire cost plus a refundable fuel deposit through the platform, and there's no unexpected excess taken from your card afterwards.
