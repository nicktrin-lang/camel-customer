## Driving in Tenerife: what UK visitors should know

Spain drives on the right, which takes most UK visitors an hour or two to settle into, especially on roundabouts where you now give way to the left. A valid UK photocard driving licence is accepted for driving in Tenerife; there's no need for an International Driving Permit for a standard holiday visit. Key things to know:

- The TF-1 and TF-5 motorways linking the south, the airport and the capital are toll-free.
- Speed limits are typically 120 km/h on motorways, 90-100 km/h on main roads, and 50 km/h in towns, all in kilometres, not miles.
- Mountain roads towards Teide National Park and the village of Masca are narrow with sharp bends, so allow extra time and drive cautiously.
- Fines for mobile phone use and drink-driving are strict and enforced by the Guardia Civil.
- Fuel stations (gasolineras) are widespread along the motorways and in resort towns.

## Best day trips from Tenerife with your hire car

Having your own car delivered to you rather than relying on excursion buses opens up the island properly:

- **Mount Teide National Park**: Spain's highest peak, roughly 45-60 minutes' drive from the southern resorts, with dramatic volcanic scenery and a cable car near the summit.
- **Masca village**: A winding mountain drive through the Teno massif to one of the island's most photographed villages.
- **Los Gigantes cliffs**: Sea cliffs on the west coast, easily combined with a stop in Puerto de Santiago.
- **La Laguna and Santa Cruz**: The old university town and the island's capital, both near Tenerife North Airport, good for a change of pace from the resorts.
- **Anaga Rural Park**: Ancient laurel forest and coastal paths in the island's northeast corner.

A car collected without a desk queue means you can start these trips early, which matters for narrow mountain roads that get busier by midday.
