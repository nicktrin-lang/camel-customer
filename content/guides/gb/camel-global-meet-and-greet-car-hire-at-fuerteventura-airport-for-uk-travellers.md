## Driving in Fuerteventura: what UK visitors should expect

Driving on the right takes most UK visitors a short adjustment period, and Fuerteventura's roads make it easier than most: traffic is light outside Puerto del Rosario and the main resort areas, roundabouts are frequent and well signposted, and the FV-1 dual carriageway running north-south from the airport is straightforward motorway-style driving. Where it gets more interesting is the wind. Fuerteventura is one of the windiest islands in the Canaries, which is exactly why it's a magnet for surfers and kitesurfers, but it also means exposed coastal roads can get gusty, particularly around the north near Corralejo and the west coast towards El Cotillo. Take corners a little steadier than you would at home, especially in a smaller hire car, and keep an eye out for wind-blown sand drifting across the tarmac near the dunes.

Minor roads into volcanic villages inland can narrow quickly and surfaces vary, so a car with reasonable ground clearance is worth considering if your itinerary includes places like Betancuria or the hills around Pájara.

## Planning a route from Corralejo to the west coast

Most UK visitors either base themselves in Corralejo in the north, close to the Corralejo Natural Park and its dramatic dune system, or in the calmer resorts further south. From FUE, Corralejo is a run of roughly half an hour up the FV-1, past Puerto del Rosario, with clear signage the whole way. From Corralejo, the west coast surf towns, El Cotillo chief among them, sit across the island via the FV-10, a route that takes you through open, lava-strewn landscape before dropping to a coastline of quieter beaches and lagoons that feel a world away from the dunes.

Having your own car delivered to you at the airport means you can head straight to whichever base suits your trip without waiting on transfer timetables, and you're free to string together dune walks, surf towns and inland volcanic villages on your own schedule rather than a coach itinerary's.
