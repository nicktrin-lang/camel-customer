## Driving in Spain: What UK Visitors Need to Know

Since Brexit, UK driving licences remain valid for tourists driving in Spain for short stays, so most visitors do not need an International Driving Permit, though it is always worth checking current requirements before you travel if your trip is longer or your circumstances are unusual. The biggest adjustment for British drivers is straightforward: Spain drives on the right. Roundabouts, junctions and dual carriageways all work in mirror image to what you are used to, so give yourself a few minutes to settle in before pulling onto busier roads.

Speed limits are shown in kilometres per hour, not miles, and typically run to 120 km/h on motorways (autovias and autopistas), 90 to 100 km/h on main roads outside towns, and 50 km/h or lower in built-up areas. Spanish law requires you to carry a reflective vest and warning triangles in the car and to use them if you break down at the roadside, and drivers must wear glasses if needed, exactly as stated on their licence. Mobile phone use while driving, even hands-free in some situations, is heavily restricted, so it is worth setting your sat nav before you set off.

## Roads Around Malaga and the Costa del Sol

Most UK visitors picking up a car near Malaga head straight for the AP-7 or A-7, the main coastal routes linking Malaga to Marbella, Estepona and beyond towards Gibraltar, or east towards Nerja and Almeria. The AP-7 is a toll motorway in parts, while the A-7 runs parallel as a free alternative, usually a little slower but perfectly manageable. If your holiday plans include a day trip inland to Ronda or the white villages of Andalucia, expect narrower, winding mountain roads with hairpin bends, so a smaller, more manoeuvrable car is often a sensible choice. Central Malaga itself has a low emission zone with restricted access for certain vehicles, so if your itinerary includes the city centre, it is worth confirming with your operator that your hire car can enter without issue.
