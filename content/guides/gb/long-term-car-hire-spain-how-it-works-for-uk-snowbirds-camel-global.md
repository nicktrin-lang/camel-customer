# Long Term Car Hire Spain: What UK Snowbirds Need to Know | Camel Global

Long term car hire in Spain works differently from booking a week at the airport desk: local operators price by the month rather than by the day, which usually works out far better value than stringing together repeated short rentals, and the car can still be delivered straight to your villa, apartment or arrival airport on the day you land. You agree a monthly rate, a mileage allowance and a fuel arrangement upfront, then the car is met and handed over wherever you're staying, with no depot trip and no queue after your flight. This guide covers pricing, delivery, fuel, mileage caps and how hiring compares with owning a car outright for part of the year.

## How does long-term car hire in Spain actually work?

Long-term car hire is simply a hire agreement structured for weeks or months rather than days. Instead of booking through a big airport counter, you request your dates and car type on a platform like Camel Global, and local car hire companies operating in your area send back their own offers for that period. You compare offers, pick one, and book. The steps are the same as a short trip, just scaled up:

1. Tell the platform your Spanish base (Costa del Sol, Costa Blanca, the Balearics, wherever your property or holiday let is), your dates, and the type of car you need.
2. Approved local operators bid for your booking with their own monthly rates.
3. You choose an offer and pay the hire cost plus a refundable fuel deposit, all handled through the platform.
4. The car is delivered and handed over to you at the airport, your villa, or another agreed address.
5. You drive for the length of your stay, then return the car at the end and the unused part of your fuel deposit is refunded.

Because you're dealing with local companies rather than a single national desk, pricing tends to reflect genuine local competition rather than one fixed seasonal rate card.

## Why do back-to-back short rentals cost snowbirds more than they realise?

Many UK second-home owners book a fresh one or two week rental every time they fly out, treating each trip as a separate transaction. This has a few hidden costs:

- Daily rates are priced for short-stay tourists, not for someone staying six, eight or twelve weeks, so the per-day cost stays high throughout.
- Every new booking means a new queue, a new counter check, and potentially a new deposit hold on your card.
- You often end up rebooking the same car type from the same limited set of companies at the airport rather than comparing what's actually available locally.
- Fuel is bought and lost repeatedly rather than managed once, over the whole stay.

A single long-term booking, by contrast, is priced and delivered once for the whole period you're in Spain, which removes the repeated overheads of restarting a rental every time.

## How is monthly pricing different from daily airport rates?

Daily airport rates are built around short tourist stays and typically include a premium for the convenience of a desk right at arrivals. Monthly rates from local operators are structured differently:

| | Repeated short-term hire | Long-term hire (monthly) |
|---|---|---|
| Pricing basis | Daily rate, booked in short blocks | Monthly rate, agreed for the full stay |
| Collection | Desk or shuttle bus each time | Delivered once to your villa or airport |
| Fuel | Bought and lost, or refilled, each booking | Managed once, refundable deposit |
| Paperwork | New agreement every trip | One agreement for the stay |
| Price competition | Limited to airport counter operators | Local companies bid for the booking |

Because the operator only has to deliver, service and reclaim the car once, rather than turning it around between short lets, they can offer a rate that reflects the longer commitment.

## Can the car still be delivered to my villa or the airport?

Yes. Meet and greet delivery isn't limited to short breaks. Whether you're flying into Malaga, Alicante, Palma or another Spanish airport, or you're already at your Spanish property and simply extending your stay, you can arrange for the car to be brought to you: at arrivals, at your villa, or at another address you specify. This means:

- No shuttle bus to an off-site depot after a long flight.
- No queue at a rental desk, whatever time your flight lands.
- The car is waiting, met and handed over at the time and place you agreed, whether that's the airport or your front door.

This matters more, not less, on a long stay: if you're settling in for two or three months, the last thing you want is to lose half a day of it standing at a counter.

## What happens with fuel policy over an extended stay?

Fuel is one of the most common sources of confusion and disputed charges in long car hire. Camel Global's model is straightforward: you pay the hire cost plus a refundable fuel deposit when you book. At the end of your hire, whether that's after two weeks or four months, the operator refunds what's left of your deposit once the fuel used has been accounted for. You are not asked to prepay for a full tank you may never use, and you don't need to time a final fuel stop to the litre before you hand the car back.

## What about mileage caps on a long-term hire?

Long-term agreements typically include a mileage allowance for the hire period, set by the local operator you book with. Because offers come from different companies, it's worth checking the mileage terms of each bid before you accept it, particularly if you plan to:

- Drive regularly between your villa and the coast, an airport, or inland towns.
- Take longer road trips within Spain during your stay.
- Use the car daily rather than occasionally, as many second-home owners do.

Comparing mileage terms alongside the monthly price is the best way to make sure the cheapest-looking offer is actually the best value for how you intend to use the car.

## Long-term car hire vs owning a car in Spain: which makes sense for part-year residents?

For UK owners who spend part of the year in Spain, the choice usually comes down to how many months you're actually there and how much admin you want to take on.

**Owning a car in Spain** means handling Spanish registration, insurance, and roadworthiness inspections (the ITV) even for the months the car sits idle, plus arranging secure storage while you're back in the UK.

**Long-term hire** removes all of that: no registration, no year-round insurance, no storage to arrange, and no car depreciating in a garage for half the year. You simply hire for the weeks or months you're actually in the country, with the car delivered when you arrive and collected when you leave.

For most part-year residents spending anywhere from a few weeks to a few months at a time, long-term hire avoids the fixed costs of ownership that apply whether or not you're actually in Spain to use the car.

## How does meet and greet car hire compare to the airport rental desk?

At a traditional airport desk, you queue to collect the car, queue again to check it back in, and the price is set by whichever counter you happen to book. With meet and greet car hire through a platform like Camel Global, local companies compete for your booking, the car is delivered to you rather than the other way round, and you can compare offers before you commit rather than accepting the first price you see. For a long stay, this also means you only go through that process once, not every time you fly out.

## What should UK drivers know about driving in Spain?

A valid UK driving licence is accepted for driving in Spain. Spanish motorways and dual carriageways are generally well maintained, but remember Spain drives on the right, speed limits are in kilometres per hour, and tolls apply on many motorways (autopistas), particularly around the Costa del Sol and Costa Blanca. If you're hiring for months rather than weeks, it's worth confirming with your chosen operator whether winter tyres or snow chains are recommended if you plan to drive inland or into mountainous areas outside the main coastal season.
