## Landing at Reina Sofía: what to expect

TFS is a single-terminal airport, so once you've collected your luggage from the belt, you're only a short walk from the main arrivals hall and the doors out to the pick-up area. Most UK flights arrive here rather than at Tenerife North (Los Rodeos), which is further from the main southern resorts and prone to more cloud and wind. The traditional rental car desks sit inside or just outside the terminal, but even a short queue at those counters, combined with paperwork and add-on upselling, can easily eat 30 to 45 minutes of your holiday before you've even left the building. With a meet and greet handover arranged through Camel Global, you agree a pick-up point and time in advance, so the driver is waiting when you land, whether your flight is on time or running a little late.

## Driving south from the airport: Costa Adeje and Los Cristianos

One advantage of flying into TFS specifically is that it sits much closer to the main southern resorts than Tenerife North does. Costa Adeje is roughly a 20 to 25 minute drive along the TF-1 motorway, and Los Cristianos is closer still, often under 15 minutes depending on traffic and time of day. The TF-1 is a well-maintained dual carriageway with clear signage in Spanish, and junction numbers are easy to follow if you've set your destination on a phone or sat-nav before leaving the car park. There are no motorway tolls anywhere in Tenerife, which is one less thing to budget for compared with driving on the Spanish mainland.

## Heading up to Teide: what UK drivers should know

A road trip up to Mount Teide National Park is one of the most popular reasons visitors hire a car in Tenerife rather than relying on excursion coaches. The roads climbing from the south, such as the TF-21 and TF-38, are well-surfaced but genuinely mountainous, with tight hairpin bends, sudden changes in visibility as you pass through cloud layers, and a noticeable drop in temperature the higher you climb. Fuel stops become sparser once you leave the coastal resorts, so it's worth filling up, or checking your fuel level, before you start the ascent. Driving here is on the right, as in mainland Spain, which takes a little adjustment for UK drivers, particularly at roundabouts and when overtaking on the motorway sections nearer the coast.
