## Seville Airport: What UK Arrivals Should Expect

Seville Airport (San Pablo, airport code SVQ) is a compact, single-terminal airport around 10 kilometres northeast of the city centre. Most direct flights from the UK, including routes from London, Manchester and Bristol, land here in the early afternoon or evening, which is exactly the window when rental desks tend to be busiest. Because the terminal is relatively small, the walk from the gate to the arrivals hall is quick, but the car hire desks inside can still back up fast during peak season, particularly over Easter (Semana Santa), the spring feria period, and the summer months when Andalusia sees heavy tourist traffic. ## Driving Out of Seville Airport: Roads and Routes

Once you're in the car, the airport connects directly onto the A-4 and the SE-30 ring road, which loops around Seville and gives you fast access towards the city centre, Cordoba to the northeast, or Cadiz and Jerez to the south. UK drivers should note that Spain drives on the right, and Seville's ring road roundabouts can come up quickly if you're used to left-hand driving, so give yourself a few minutes to settle in before pushing on. If your route takes you into Seville's historic centre, be aware of the Zona de Bajas Emisiones (ZBE), a low-emission zone covering much of the old town where access is restricted for certain vehicles, and streets around Santa Cruz and the Cathedral are narrow, often pedestrianised, and not built for a hire car. Most road-trippers heading into Andalusia bypass the old town by car altogether and either park on the outskirts or push straight on towards Ronda, the white villages of the Sierra, or the Cadiz coast.
