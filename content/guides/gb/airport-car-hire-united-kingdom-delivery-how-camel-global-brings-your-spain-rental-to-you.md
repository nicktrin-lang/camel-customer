## What should UK drivers know about driving in Spain?

A UK driving licence is valid for driving in Spain as a visitor, but there are some rules that catch UK drivers out because they're different from home:

- **You drive on the right.** This takes conscious adjustment, especially at roundabouts and junctions in the first day or two.
- **Distances and speeds are in kilometres.** Motorway limits are typically higher-numbered than UK motorways because they're in km/h, not mph, so check the dashboard display rather than relying on habit.
- **Some motorways are tolled (autopistas de peaje)**, particularly around the coast and between major cities, while others are free (autovías).
- **Spanish law requires you to carry a reflective vest and a warning triangle in the car**, and to use them if you break down on certain roads.
- **Drink-drive limits in Spain are stricter than in England and Wales**, so it's worth being cautious even after modest drinking.
- **On-the-spot fines can be issued** for common infringements, so it helps to know local rules before setting off rather than after.

Your local car hire operator, delivering the car in person, is often a useful source for quick, practical advice about the specific roads around your arrival point.

## How does the car hire fuel policy work, and how do I avoid hidden fees?

A common source of unexpected cost in car hire is the fuel policy. Many traditional rentals ask you to buy a full tank upfront, meaning you lose the value of any fuel you don't burn. With Camel Global, you instead leave a refundable fuel deposit when you collect the car. When you return it, the local operator calculates what fuel you actually used, and the rest of your deposit is refunded. To avoid hidden fees generally when hiring a car in Spain, always check what's included in the quoted price, understand the fuel arrangement before you sign anything, ask what happens with tolls if you plan to use tolled motorways, and keep a copy of the condition report when you collect and return the vehicle.
