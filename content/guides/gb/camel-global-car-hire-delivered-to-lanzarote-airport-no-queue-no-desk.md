## Driving Out of Arrecife Towards the Volcanoes

Lanzarote's road network is refreshingly manageable for anyone used to UK motorways. From the airport, the LZ-2 and LZ-30 give quick access south towards Yaiza and the entrance to Timanfaya National Park, home to the island's volcanic trails and the eerie, rust-coloured landscape that inspired Manrique's own fascination with the island. Roads here are generally well surfaced, and roundabouts are common rather than traffic lights, which UK drivers usually adjust to within a day. Remember that Spain drives on the right, so roundabout etiquette runs the opposite way to home, worth a moment's thought before you pull out of the airport car park for the first time. Fuel stations are straightforward, and unleaded and diesel are both widely available, though it's sensible to fill up before heading into quieter stretches near the national park where stations are sparser.

## Into the Wine Valleys of La Geria

A short drive north east of Timanfaya brings you into La Geria, where vines are grown in individual volcanic pits protected by low stone walls, a method developed specifically to trap moisture in this arid landscape. The LZ-30 threads directly through the valley, and having your own car here matters more than almost anywhere else on the island: bus services are limited, and the bodegas worth stopping at are spread out along quiet rural roads rather than clustered in one town. Having a car delivered straight to you at the airport means you can be tasting Malvasía wine within the hour rather than losing half a day to logistics.

Manrique's influence shapes the whole island, from the whitewashed building codes to landmark sites like Jameos del Agua and the Mirador del Río in the north. Coastal roads can be narrow and winding in places, particularly around the north, so it's worth taking these sections at a gentler pace than the main LZ routes.
