## What UK Travellers Need to Know Before Driving in Spain

A full UK driving licence is generally accepted for hire and driving in Spain for visits of this length, but it's worth carrying your passport and licence together, as local operators will want to see both alongside the payment card used for the booking. Spain drives on the right, which takes a little adjustment on your first roundabout, particularly around the airport approach roads. Speed limits are posted in kilometres per hour, and Spanish motorways (autopistas) often carry tolls, notably the AP-7 that runs along much of the coast. The free alternative, the N-332, is slower but hugs the coastline and can be a scenic way to reach some of the Costa de Valencia towns if you're not in a rush.

Valencia city itself operates a low emission zone in parts of the centre, so if your route takes you through the old town, it's worth checking current access rules before you drive in, particularly with an older hire car.

## Getting to the Albufera Natural Park

The Albufera Natural Park sits just south of Valencia city, easily reached in around twenty to thirty minutes by car from the airport, making it a natural first stop for a road trip. The freshwater lagoon, surrounded by rice paddies that produce much of the region's paella rice, is best explored slowly, with stops at the fishing village of El Palmar and the lookout points around the lake for sunset. Having your own car here is a genuine advantage over relying on tours or buses, since the park's back roads and small villages aren't always well served by public transport, and a hire car delivered straight to the airport means you can be exploring the lagoon within the hour rather than losing your first afternoon to admin.

## Exploring the Costa de Valencia by Road

Heading north or south from the airport opens up the wider Costa de Valencia, from the beaches around El Saler close to the Albufera, up through Valencia's own city beaches, and further afield towards towns like Sagunto or down towards Cullera and Gandia. A hire car gives you the freedom to string together beach stops, old town wanders and coastal viewpoints in whatever order suits your trip, rather than working around fixed coach timetables. Parking in the smaller coastal towns is generally easier than in the city centre, though August and peak summer weekends can still get busy, so arriving reasonably early in the day helps.
