## Getting to the South Coast Resorts

Most UK holidaymakers heading to Gran Canaria are bound for the south, and the drive from LPA to Maspalomas, Playa del Inglés or Puerto Rico is one of the easier airport transfers in the Canary Islands. The GC-1 motorway runs almost directly from the airport down the eastern and southern coast, and the journey to Maspalomas typically takes around thirty five to forty minutes in normal traffic, slightly longer if you're continuing further west towards Puerto Rico or Puerto de Mogán. The road is dual carriageway for most of the route, well signposted, and toll-free, so it's a straightforward first drive even if you've never driven on the right before. The main thing to keep in mind is that overtaking lanes can end without much warning near the exits for Playa del Inglés, so it pays to stay alert rather than assume the motorway continues at full width the whole way.

## Heading Inland Into the Mountains

Gran Canaria's interior is a completely different driving experience from the coastal motorway, and it's one of the main reasons visitors choose to hire a car rather than rely on resort transfers. Routes up towards Tejeda, Artenara or the Roque Nublo area involve narrow, winding mountain roads with frequent hairpin bends and significant changes in altitude within a short distance. These roads are well maintained but demand a slower pace, particularly if you're not used to driving in mountainous terrain, and a smaller or mid-sized car is generally easier to manage than something bulky. Fuel stops become less frequent once you're away from the coast, so it's worth filling up before you start climbing. If your holiday plan mixes beach days in the south with day trips inland, having your own car delivered directly to you at the airport means you're not tied to fixed excursion times and can set off whenever suits you.

## What to Know Before You Land

UK licences are accepted for driving in Spain, and you won't need an international driving permit for a standard car hire. Roads are signed in kilometres and speed limits are lower than many UK drivers expect, particularly through village centres on the inland routes, so it's worth paying attention to signage rather than driving on instinct. Parking in Las Palmas and the busier resort centres can be limited, so many visitors prefer to leave the car at their accommodation and walk into town centres where possible.
