# Car Hire Delivered to Málaga Airport for a Córdoba Road Trip: The Camel Global Guide

Málaga Airport is where most British holidaymakers land in southern Spain, and most of them never leave the coast. The Costa del Sol has its pull: beaches, resort towns, a steady stream of direct flights from every corner of the UK. Meanwhile Córdoba, just under two hours inland, sits quietly with one of the finest historic centres in Europe and a fraction of the coastal crowds. The question worth asking before you book anything is simple: do you land at Málaga and stay on the coast, or do you land at Málaga and push on to Córdoba? The honest answer is that it depends on your trip, but the logistics of getting a car either way matter more than most people realise, and that is where a meet and greet car hire delivered straight to you changes the calculation.

## Why Córdoba Gets Skipped

Córdoba doesn't have its own major international airport, so it survives on being a day trip or a detour from Málaga, Seville or Madrid. That means anyone visiting has to make an active decision to go there, rather than simply landing and arriving. Most people on a one-week Costa del Sol holiday never make that decision. They land at Málaga, transfer to a resort, and the inland cities become something they meant to see but didn't. Given that Córdoba's old town, mosque-cathedral and narrow whitewashed streets are genuinely worth the detour, it's a shame how often it gets left off the itinerary purely because the coast is the path of least resistance.

## The Route and Drive Times

From Málaga Airport to Córdoba, the drive is straightforward: mostly motorway (the A-45), running north through the mountains before the landscape opens into the Guadalquivir valley. Expect around one hour forty-five minutes to two hours depending on traffic and where in Córdoba you're headed. It's a genuinely scenic drive, particularly once you clear the Málaga hinterland and start seeing olive groves stretch out either side of the road.

If you're staying on the coast instead, journeys are much shorter and more localised: Marbella is around 45 minutes from the airport, Fuengirola and Torremolinos are 15 to 30 minutes, Estepona is closer to an hour. The contrast matters when you're deciding what kind of hire car and pickup arrangement makes sense. A short coastal hop doesn't need the same planning as a proper inland road trip.

## Driving in Córdoba Itself

Córdoba's historic centre is largely pedestrianised, with narrow streets that were never designed for cars, let alone rental estates. Most visitors park on the outskirts of the old town and walk in, so don't expect to drive right up to the mosque-cathedral. Outside the centre, roads are ordinary Spanish city streets, well signed and easy enough for anyone used to UK driving once you remember to keep right. Fuel stations are common on the approach roads, useful if you're returning the car with a specific amount of fuel in mind.
