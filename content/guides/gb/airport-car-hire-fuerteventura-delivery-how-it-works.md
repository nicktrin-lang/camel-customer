## What should UK drivers know about driving in Fuerteventura?

Driving in Fuerteventura is generally easier than in mainland Spain's cities, but a few local points matter for UK licence holders:

- You drive on the right, and priority at roundabouts is usually to traffic already on the roundabout, same as at home, though signage can differ.
- A full, valid UK driving licence is accepted; no international driving permit is normally needed for a short UK holiday visit, but check current requirements before you travel.
- The FV-1 dual carriageway runs along much of the east coast connecting the airport, Puerto del Rosario, Caleta de Fuste, Costa Calma and Morro Jable, and is generally well maintained and quiet compared with UK motorways.
- Roads into the interior and towards Betancuria, Pájara and the west coast are narrower, winding, and cross volcanic terrain, so allow extra time.
- Petrol stations are self-service in places and staffed in others; unleaded is sold as "gasolina sin plomo 95" and diesel as "gasóleo" or "diésel", so check which your hire car needs before filling up.
- Fierce crosswinds are common on exposed stretches, particularly near Corralejo and along the FV-1, so keep a firm grip on the wheel, especially in a smaller or taller vehicle.

## What are good day trips from Fuerteventura by hire car?

Having your own car opens up the island well beyond your resort:

- **Corralejo Dunes Natural Park**: golden dune landscapes a short drive north of Corralejo, with parking right by the beach.
- **El Cotillo**: quieter north-west coast village with lagoons and dramatic sunsets, roughly 40 minutes from Corralejo.
- **Betancuria**: the island's old inland capital, set in a mountain valley, a scenic drive from either the north or the airport area.
- **Ajuy**: black sand beach and sea caves on the west coast, popular for walkers and photographers.
- **Jandía Natural Park and Cofete beach**: in the far south, reached via an unpaved track that suits a higher-clearance vehicle.
- **Ferry to Lobos Island or Lanzarote**: both depart from Corralejo, so a car gets you to the harbour on your own schedule.
