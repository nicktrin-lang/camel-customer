## What should UK drivers know about driving in and around Madrid?

- **Documents**: bring your full UK driving licence; an International Driving Permit isn't required for most standard UK licences on short trips to Spain, but check current requirements before you travel.
- **Low emissions zone**: central Madrid operates a ZBE. Combustion cars generally need a valid environmental badge or registration to enter certain central districts, so check this before booking a hotel deep in the centre.
- **Parking**: on-street bays in most central neighbourhoods are metered SER zones during the day. Many hotels offer paid parking, which is often simpler than hunting for a bay.
- **Ring roads**: the M-30 circles the city and the M-40 further out; both get busy at rush hour, so build in extra time if you're driving out for a day trip in the late afternoon.
- **Roundabouts and priority**: Spanish roundabouts give priority to traffic already on the roundabout, and city driving involves more roundabouts than many UK drivers are used to.

## What day trips make most sense with a car delivered to your Madrid hotel?

- **Toledo**: around an hour's drive south, a compact walled city with narrow streets best explored on foot once you park outside the old town.
- **Segovia**: roughly an hour north-west via the motorway, known for its Roman aqueduct and cathedral.
- **Aranjuez**: about 45 minutes south, home to a royal palace and gardens, an easy half-day trip.
- **Chinchón**: under an hour south-east, a small town built around a historic arcaded square.
- **Sierra de Guadarrama**: for those wanting mountain scenery within reach of the city.

For any of these, a car collected at your hotel that morning, rather than one sitting in a car park for your whole stay, is usually the more practical option.
