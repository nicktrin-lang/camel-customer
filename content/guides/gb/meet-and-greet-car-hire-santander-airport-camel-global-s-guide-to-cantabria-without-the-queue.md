# Meet and Greet Car Hire Santander Airport with Camel Global

Santander Airport (officially Seve Ballesteros-Santander Airport) has quietly become one of the most useful little airports for UK travellers heading into Cantabria and eastern Asturias. Budget carriers now run regular routes from several UK cities, and the appeal is obvious: you land, and within twenty minutes you could be looking at the Bay of Biscay rather than a motorway service station. The catch is that the terminal is small, and so is the rental car area. On busy summer mornings, when two or three flights land close together, the desks fill up fast and the queue can eat the best part of your first hour in Spain. Meet and greet car hire santander airport bookings through Camel Global are designed to remove that bottleneck entirely, so your holiday starts the moment you walk out of arrivals rather than after you have shuffled through a rental queue.

## Why the desk queue is worse than it looks

Santander's terminal building is compact by design, built to handle regional and low-cost traffic rather than the volume of a major hub. That is generally a good thing: baggage arrives quickly and the walk from the gate to the exit is short. But it also means the on-site rental counters have limited staff and limited desk space. If your flight lands alongside another arrival, or if the desk is dealing with a handful of complex bookings ahead of you, a supposedly ten-minute pickup can easily become forty-five minutes of standing in a line with your suitcase. For a family with young children, or anyone who has an evening booking at a rural casona further into Cantabria, that lost time matters. With Camel Global, there is no desk to find and no queue to join. You arrange your car online before you fly, a local operator confirms the booking, and the car is brought to meet you at arrivals so you can walk straight to it.

## Driving out of the airport and into Cantabria

Santander Airport sits close to the city, right by the bay, and the road network from there is straightforward even for a first-timer. The A-67 and S-10 give quick access both towards Santander itself and out west towards the rest of Cantabria, so you are not immediately thrown onto complicated urban streets. Cantabrian roads are generally well maintained, though once you leave the main routes for the coastal villages or head up into the Picos de Europa foothills, expect narrower lanes, hairpin bends and the occasional herd of cattle with right of way. Fuel stations are common along the main roads but thinner once you are deep into the hills, so it is worth filling up before you head west.

## A route worth planning: Altamira to the western Picos

A popular loop from Santander takes in the Altamira Museum, close to the original cave paintings, then the clifftop village of Santillana del Mar with its cobbled streets and medieval stonework, before continuing west towards the dramatic limestone peaks of the western Picos de Europa. If you would rather stay coastal, Somo's surf beaches are a short hop from the airport and a favourite for a first or last day of the trip. Having your own car, delivered directly to you, makes this kind of flexible touring realistic in a way that public transport in rural Cantabria simply is not.
