## What child seats can be pre-fitted, and how do you specify what you need?

Spanish law, in line with EU rules, requires children under 135cm in height to use an appropriate child restraint, and this applies to hire cars exactly as it does to any vehicle. When you submit your request through Camel Global, specify:

- **Age and approximate weight or height of each child**, so the operator brings the correct category (infant carrier, rear-facing seat, forward-facing seat with harness, or a high-back booster).
- **How many seats you need.** A family with a toddler and a school-age child, for example, typically needs one seat with harness and one booster.
- **ISOFIX preference**, if your children are used to an ISOFIX-fitted seat rather than a belt-fitted one.

Because a local operator prepares the specific vehicle for your booking rather than pulling a random car from a depot fleet, the seats can be fitted before the handover, so you're not doing it yourself in an airport car park with a flight-lagged toddler under one arm.

## Driving from Marbella: roads, day trips and what UK parents should know

Spain drives on the right, and your hire car will be a left-hand-drive Spanish-registered vehicle, which takes most UK drivers a short adjustment period, particularly on roundabouts. A few practical points for the Marbella area:

- **AP-7 vs A-7**: the AP-7 is a toll motorway running along the coast and is generally faster and quieter than the free A-7, which passes through more built-up stretches and can be slow in summer traffic.
- **Speed limits** are in kilometres per hour: typically 120km/h on motorways, 90-100km/h on main roads, and 50km/h in towns unless signed otherwise.
- **Mountain roads to Ronda** wind through the Sierra de las Nieves and are scenic but narrow in places, worth a slower pace with children in the car.
- **Puerto Banús and central Marbella** have restricted and metered parking, so check your hotel's parking arrangement before you drive in.

Popular family day trips from Marbella by car include Ronda (around an hour), Gibraltar (about an hour, remember passports for the border), Mijas Pueblo (20-30 minutes), and Nerja with its beaches and caves (around an hour). Granada is further, roughly two hours, and better suited to families wanting a full day out rather than a half-day loop. If your trip includes a one-way leg, for instance flying into Málaga but departing from a different city, ask operators about one way car hire options when you submit your request, since not every local company offers this and it's easier to arrange before you book than to change afterwards.
