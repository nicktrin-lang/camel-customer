## Why Is Automatic Car Hire So Hard to Find in Spain?

Manual gearboxes remain the standard across Spain, including at Alicante Airport, because that's what the local and wider European market drives day to day. Rental fleets are built around demand, so operators stock far more manual cars than automatics. For UK drivers used to automatics being common at home, this comes as a surprise.

The practical effect is that automatic cars at Alicante Airport:

- Sell out earlier than manuals, particularly from Easter through to October
- Often cost more to hire because supply is smaller relative to demand
- May be limited to a narrower range of models and sizes
- Can be unavailable altogether if you book only a few days before travel

If an automatic matters to you, booking well ahead of your trip, and being open to comparing several local operators instead of just one, makes a real difference.

## What Does Meet & Greet Car Hire at Alicante Airport Actually Involve?

Meet and greet car hire means the car comes to you rather than you going to collect it. At Alicante Airport, this typically means the local operator hands the car over at or near arrivals, at your hotel if you're staying nearby, or at another agreed location, rather than sending you to a rental desk followed by a shuttle bus to an off-site depot.

This matters for anyone arriving on a UK flight tired, with children, or with luggage, since it removes the counter queue and the wait for a courtesy bus that's common with traditional airport car hire.

## Meet and Greet Car Hire vs the Airport Rental Desk: Which Is Better?

Both get you a car, but the process differs:

- **Airport rental desk**: you queue at the counter, then often take a shuttle bus to a depot to collect the car, and repeat the process in reverse on your return flight.
- **Meet and greet with Camel Global**: you request your car in advance, compare offers from local operators, and the car is delivered to you directly, with the same happening in reverse when you return it.

Whether meet and greet is worth it depends on what you value. If avoiding queues, shuttle buses, and desk waiting time after a flight matters to you, meet and greet removes those steps. It also means you've already compared offers and agreed a price before you land, rather than negotiating extras at a counter.

## Driving in Spain: What UK Visitors Should Know

A few practical points for UK drivers heading to Alicante:

- Spain drives on the right, which takes a short adjustment period, particularly at roundabouts and junctions.
- Your UK driving licence is valid for driving in Spain; carry it with you at all times, along with your passport.
- Speed limits are in kilometres per hour, and are usually well signposted, but it's worth checking before you set off if you're not used to reading them at speed.
- Tolls apply on some motorways (autopistas) around the Costa Blanca region; free alternative routes are usually available but slower.
- Fuel stations are widely available along the coast and near Alicante Airport, so refuelling before you return the car is straightforward.
