# Meet and Greet Car Hire Jerez Airport: Skip the Desk with Camel Global

Meet and greet car hire at Jerez Airport means your hire car is brought straight to you at arrivals, so you never queue at a rental desk or take a shuttle to an off-site depot. With Camel Global, local car hire companies in the Jerez area bid for your booking, you pick the offer you like, and you pay the hire cost plus a refundable fuel deposit, getting back whatever fuel you didn't use once you return the car. It's a straightforward way to start a Costa de la Luz road trip without losing time at the airport.

## Why does meet and greet car hire make sense at Jerez Airport?

Jerez Airport (XRY) is a compact, single-terminal airport a few kilometres northeast of Jerez de la Frontera. It's small by design, which is generally good news for arriving passengers, but it also means the airport rental desks can get busy around the handful of peak flight banks that serve UK routes, and there isn't a large depot complex to absorb overflow. With meet and greet car hire, you avoid that bottleneck completely: your car is delivered to arrivals, handed over on the spot, and you're driving out of the airport approach road within minutes of collecting your luggage. There's no shuttle bus, no walk to a separate car park, and no standing in a queue behind other flights that landed around the same time as yours.

## How does the process work from booking to driving off?

1. Go to camel-global.com and enter your Jerez arrival details: dates, times and the type of car you need.

5. Drive off and start your Costa de la Luz route.
6. Return the car as agreed, and Camel Global refunds whatever part of your fuel deposit you didn't use.

## What should UK travellers know about driving from Jerez Airport?

UK licences are valid for driving in Spain, and driving is on the right, which takes most British drivers a short adjustment period, particularly at roundabouts and when pulling out of the airport junction onto the A-4 motorway. From Jerez Airport it's a very short hop, generally well under fifteen minutes in normal traffic, into Jerez de la Frontera itself via the A-4 or the local Jerez ring road. Spanish motorways (autovías, marked A) are toll-free in this part of Andalusia, and road signage around Jerez, El Puerto de Santa María and Sanlúcar de Barrameda is generally clear, with town names sign-posted well in advance.

A few practical points for this specific route:

- Town centres in the Sherry Triangle, especially Jerez and Sanlúcar, have narrow one-way streets and limited on-street parking, so a smaller car is easier to manage if you're planning to explore historic centres by car before parking up.
- The drive from Jerez to El Puerto de Santa María and on to Sanlúcar de Barrameda follows well-maintained local roads (the A-480 corridor and coastal connections), rarely more than 20 to 30 minutes between towns.
- Continuing south to Vejer de la Frontera and Tarifa means joining the A-48/N-340 coastal route, a scenic but sometimes windy stretch, which matters if you're driving a smaller hire car near Tarifa's exposed beaches.
- Fuel stations are frequent along this corridor, so managing your fuel deposit and topping up before returning the car is straightforward.

## What does a typical Sherry Triangle to Tarifa route look like?

Many travellers landing at Jerez use the airport as the natural starting point for a loop through Andalusia's sherry-producing towns before heading further south. A common structure:

- **Jerez de la Frontera**: bodegas, flamenco heritage, and the airport itself.
- **El Puerto de Santa María**: a short drive west, known for its bay-side setting and seafood.
- **Sanlúcar de Barrameda**: at the mouth of the Guadalquivir river, with its own sherry tradition and beach front.
- **Vejer de la Frontera**: a white hill town further south, worth the detour for its hilltop streets.
- **Tarifa**: the southernmost point, known for kitesurfing beaches and views across to Africa.

With a delivered car, you can adjust this route on the fly, since there's no need to return to a specific depot at a fixed time unless your booking terms say otherwise.
