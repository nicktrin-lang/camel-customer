## Arriving at Ibiza Airport: What UK Travellers Should Expect

Ibiza Airport (IBZ) sits around seven kilometres south of Ibiza Town, just off the ME-1 motorway, and it's compact compared with the mainland gateways UK travellers might be used to. There's a single terminal building handling both the summer charter crush and the quieter winter schedule, so arrivals halls can get busy fast when several flights from the UK land close together, particularly on Friday and Saturday afternoons in peak season. Traditional rental desks are grouped in one area, and in July and August the queues there can eat into your first evening on the island. With meet and greet car hire, you skip that altogether. ## Driving in Ibiza: Roads, Roundabouts and Rural Lanes

Ibiza's road network is small but varied. The main routes connecting Ibiza Town, San Antonio and Santa Eulalia are well surfaced dual carriageways with plenty of roundabouts, the standard layout across Spain, so give way to traffic already on the roundabout and take your time reading the exit signs if you're not used to driving on the right. Away from these main roads, things narrow quickly. Lanes into the hills around Sant Joan and Santa Gertrudis are single track in places, shared with mopeds, cyclists and the occasional herd of sheep, and the tracks down to some of the best beaches, like Cala d'en Serra or the approach to Es Vedra viewpoint, are unpaved and best tackled slowly. Parking in Ibiza Town's old quarter, Dalt Vila, is tight and largely restricted, with narrow one-way streets not designed for cars at all, so it's worth using one of the car parks near the marina and walking in. Fuel stations are plentiful on the main roads but scarcer in the north, so it's sensible to fill up before heading into the hills.

## Where the Island Opens Up Beyond the Clubs

A hire car turns Ibiza from a resort holiday into a proper road trip. Head north from the airport and within forty minutes you can be at Portinatx or Cala Xarraca, both a world away from the noise of the south coast strip. Santa Gertrudis, roughly in the island's centre, makes a good lunch stop with its shaded square and art galleries. Further west, the road out towards Cala Comte and the viewpoint over Es Vedra rewards an early start, before the coach parties arrive. If you're travelling on a Wednesday or Saturday, the Las Dalias hippy market near San Carlos is easily reached by car and much harder to get to without one. None of this is realistic if you're tied to a shuttle bus timetable or a full afternoon lost queuing at a rental counter.
