## Driving in Malaga and on Andalusian Roads: What UK Drivers Should Know

Spain drives on the right, which takes a short adjustment period for UK drivers, particularly at roundabouts and when pulling out of junctions. A few practical points worth knowing before you set off from Malaga:

- **Speed limits** are typically 120 km/h on motorways (autopistas and autovías), 90-100 km/h on main roads, and 30-50 km/h in built-up areas. Signs are in kilometres, so it's worth resetting your mental sense of pace.
- **Tolls** exist on some motorways around Malaga and the Costa del Sol, including sections of the AP-7. The free A-7 runs parallel in many places and is busier but toll-free, useful to know if you're watching costs.
- **Documents to carry** include your UK driving licence, passport, and the hire agreement. It's sensible to keep a photo of these on your phone as a backup.
- **Low emission zones (ZBEs)** are being introduced in various Spanish cities, and Malaga has been developing its own restrictions in parts of the centre. Check current signage or your operator's guidance if you plan to drive into the old town.
- **Parking** in central Malaga and along the coast often means paid zones (marked by blue lines) or underground car parks rather than free on-street spaces, especially in high season.

None of this is especially daunting, but knowing it before you land makes the first hour on the road far less stressful.
