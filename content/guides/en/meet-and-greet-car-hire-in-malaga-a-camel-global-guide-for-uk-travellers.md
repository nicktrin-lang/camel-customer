## Driving in Spain: What UK Visitors Need to Know

Once you've collected your car, driving in Spain has a few differences worth knowing before you pull out of the airport. Spain drives on the right, which takes most UK visitors a little concentration for the first few junctions, particularly at roundabouts where the flow is the opposite of what you're used to at home. Motorways around Malaga, including the AP-7 toll road along the coast and the free A-7, are generally well maintained, though the A-7 through Marbella and Fuengirola can get heavily congested in peak season, so building in extra time is sensible. Speed limits are posted in kilometres per hour, not miles, and are strictly enforced with fixed cameras, particularly on approaches to towns. It's a legal requirement to carry a reflective vest and warning triangle in the car, along with your UK driving licence, and to always drive with headlights on in tunnels. Parking in central Malaga and towns like Nerja is often restricted to blue zones requiring a ticket, so it's worth checking signage carefully rather than guessing.
