## Why El Prat's Layout Makes Meet and Greet Worth Considering

Barcelona-El Prat has two main terminals, T1 and T2, connected by a shuttle bus that takes around ten to fifteen minutes, plus waiting time. Most UK arrivals, whether on easyJet, Vueling, British Airways or Ryanair, will land at one or the other depending on the route. The traditional rental car area sits away from both terminals, meaning that after baggage collection you're often looking at another bus journey before you even reach a rental counter, and that's before the paperwork and queueing begins.

With a delivery-based booking, that extra leg disappears. You arrange a pickup point at arrivals with the local operator handling your booking, and the car is brought to you there. It's a genuinely useful difference when you've landed after a delayed flight, when you're travelling with young kids, or when you simply want to be on the AP-7 motorway towards the coast within the hour rather than still standing in a terminal.

## Driving Out of Barcelona: Montserrat, Sitges and the Costa Brava

Once you've got the keys, Catalonia opens up quickly. Montserrat, with its dramatic mountain monastery, is around an hour's drive north-west via the C-16, and the roads are well-surfaced motorway for most of the way before winding up into the hills. Sitges, the popular coastal town south of the city, is a straightforward 35-minute run down the C-32 toll road, and makes a relaxed first stop if you're easing into left-hand driving. Heading the other way, the Costa Brava towns like Begur, Cadaqués and Tossa de Mar sit two to two and a half hours north up the AP-7, with tolls on several stretches, so it's worth keeping some coins or a card handy at the barriers.

Remember that Spain drives on the right, speed limits are in kilometres per hour, and UK licences are accepted for driving in Spain, though it's worth carrying your paper counterpart or checking current requirements before you travel.

## Paying Only for the Fuel You Use

Rather than being pushed into buying a full tank upfront and losing whatever's left at drop-off, Camel Global's model works on a refundable fuel deposit. You pay it when you book, and once you return the car, you're refunded everything except the fuel you actually used. It's a fairer way to handle something that traditionally catches travellers out.
