## Driving in Spain: What UK Visitors Should Know Before Setting Off

A few practical points for UK drivers heading out on Spanish roads:

- Spain drives on the right, the opposite side to the UK, so take extra care at junctions and roundabouts, especially in the first few miles.
- Motorway speed limits are generally 120 km/h, dropping to 90-100 km/h on other main roads and 30-50 km/h in towns; signs are in kilometres, not miles.
- Many motorways (autopistas) are toll roads, while autovias are usually free; keep a card handy for toll booths.
- Several Spanish cities, including Madrid and Barcelona, operate low emission zones with entry restrictions, so check requirements before driving into the centre.
- UK driving licences and current entry requirements can change, so check the latest rules for driving in Spain before you travel.
- Spanish law requires you to carry certain items in the car, such as a reflective vest and warning triangles; ask your operator what's already provided.

- One-way car hire: if you're flying into one city and out of another, mention this, as not every operator supports it.
- Long-term car hire: for extended stays, ask operators to quote for the full length of your trip rather than piecing together short bookings.
