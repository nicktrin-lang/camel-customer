## Why the Rental Desk Queue Catches So Many UK Travellers Out

Malaga Airport (AGP) is one of the busiest gateways in Spain, particularly during British half-term weeks and the summer season when several flights from Manchester, Gatwick, Stansted and Bristol land within the same hour. The car hire desks sit in the arrivals area and at the dedicated car hire centre a short walk or shuttle ride from Terminal 3, and on a busy afternoon the queue for the big-name counters can easily eat into the first part of your day. Add in the extra kiosk stop to "confirm" your booking, the upsell conversation, and the walk out to a distant depot to actually collect the vehicle, and a tired family can lose the best part of an hour before they've left the airport perimeter. A meet and greet approach cuts that out entirely: no depot, no second queue, no shuttle bus.

## Fuel: You Only Pay a Deposit, Not a Full Tank

One detail that catches out a lot of first-time visitors to Spain is the fuel policy. Traditional counters often want you to buy a full tank upfront, whether or not you'll use it. Through Camel Global, you instead leave a refundable fuel deposit when you collect the car. When you return it, the operator settles up based on what you've actually used, and refunds the rest of the deposit back to you. It's a simpler, fairer way of handling fuel than guessing how full the tank needs to be when you drop the car back near Puerto Banus or back at the airport.

## Driving Out of Malaga: Marbella and Fuengirola

Once you're behind the wheel, the AP-7 and A-7 motorways run west along the coast, and Marbella is roughly 45 minutes to an hour away depending on traffic, with Fuengirola closer still at around 25 to 30 minutes. Both routes are well signposted and easy for UK drivers used to driving on the right, though it's worth remembering that Spanish motorways have tolls on some stretches (the AP-7 is toll-free along most of this coastal section, but always check current signage), and speed limits are stricter on urban approach roads than they first appear. Marbella's old town has narrow, largely pedestrianised streets, so it's worth booking a car with a size that suits where you'll actually be parking, whether that's a hotel car park or a public car park just outside the centre.

## Heading Inland to Ronda and the White Villages

For a different side of Andalusia, the A-397 climbs up through pine forest towards Ronda, a journey of around an hour and a half from Malaga that winds through some genuinely spectacular mountain scenery. The road has plenty of bends, so it rewards a car you feel comfortable in rather than the cheapest option regardless of size. Ronda itself, with its gorge-spanning bridge, and the surrounding white villages like Casares and Gaucin, are far easier to enjoy with your own car than on a coach tour, since you can stop wherever the view demands it and set your own pace back down to the coast.
