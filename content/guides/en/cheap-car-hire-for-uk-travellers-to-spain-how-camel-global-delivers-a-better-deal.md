## What should UK drivers know about driving in Spain?

Spain drives on the right, which is the main adjustment for UK drivers used to right-hand-drive cars and left-hand traffic. Practical points to know before you go:

- You'll need your full UK driving licence; if you passed your test before 1997 you may also need an International Driving Permit, so check current requirements before travel.
- Speed limits are in kilometres per hour, typically 120 km/h on motorways (autopistas/autovías), 90 to 100 km/h on other roads, and 20 to 50 km/h in towns.
- Many Spanish motorways have tolls (peajes), particularly around the Costa del Sol, Catalonia and the Basque Country; free alternative roads usually run alongside them but take longer.
- Spanish law requires you to carry two reflective warning triangles and a reflective jacket in the car, along with spare bulbs in some regions.
- Drink driving limits are stricter than in the UK, and fines for phone use while driving are issued on the spot.
- Roundabout priority and give-way rules differ slightly from home, so take it slowly on your first junctions.

## How does the fuel policy work, and how do I avoid hidden fees?

A lot of car hire frustration comes down to fuel. The common approach elsewhere is to make you buy a full tank upfront, whether you need it or not, and you lose whatever's left when you return the car. Camel Global's approach is different: you leave a refundable fuel deposit alongside the hire cost, and once you bring the car back, you're refunded everything except the fuel you actually used.

To avoid hidden fees generally when hiring a car in Spain:

- Read the offer details before booking, not after you land.
- Confirm exactly what excess or deposit terms apply before you agree.
- Check what's included, such as delivery to your terminal or hotel, and what handover point is agreed in advance.
- Keep your booking confirmation and any communication with the operator in case questions arise on return.
