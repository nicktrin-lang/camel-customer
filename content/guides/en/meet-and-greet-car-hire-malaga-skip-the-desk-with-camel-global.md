## Driving in Spain: What UK Visitors Should Know

Once you've got the keys, a few practical differences from UK roads are worth keeping in mind. Spain drives on the right, which takes most people an hour or so to adjust to, particularly at roundabouts and junctions. Motorways (autopistas and autovías) around Malaga are generally well maintained, and the AP-7 toll road along the coast is a faster, quieter alternative to the free A-7 if you're heading towards Marbella or Estepona.

Spanish law requires you to carry your driving licence, and a valid passport, at all times while driving, along with the car's documents, which your operator will provide. Speed limits are posted in kilometres per hour, not miles, so it's worth resetting your mental sense of pace, particularly in town centres where 30km/h zones are common and enforced. Fines for mobile phone use while driving are strict, and using a hands-free system is expected if you need to take a call.

Parking in central Malaga can be tight, and many areas use blue or green zone paid parking during the day, so check signage carefully or use a car park if you're stopping in the city itself.
