## Landing at Vigo-Peinador: what to expect

Vigo Airport (officially Vigo-Peinador, airport code VGO) is a compact, single-terminal regional airport around nine kilometres north-east of the city centre. There's no rail link, so most arriving passengers either take a taxi, a local bus, or arrange to be met with a hire car. Because the terminal is small, arrivals and the kerb outside are only a short walk apart, which makes meet and greet handover genuinely convenient here: you land, collect your luggage, and your car can be brought to you at an agreed spot near the terminal rather than you having to trek to an off-site depot. For anyone arriving on a UK flight after an early start or a delayed departure, that difference matters more than it sounds.

## Driving the AP-9 and the coast roads south to Baiona

From Vigo, the AP-9 Autopista del Atlántico is the fast toll motorway running the length of Galicia's coast, useful if you're heading up towards Pontevedra or Santiago. For Baiona, most drivers use the PO-552 coast road, a scenic route hugging the Ría de Vigo that takes around 30 to 40 minutes depending on traffic through Nigrán. Baiona's old town is tight and largely pedestrianised around the Monterreal fortress, so plan to park just outside the historic centre and walk in rather than trying to drive to your door. Toll costs on the AP-9 are modest but worth having some coins or a card ready for, and Galicia's roads are generally well maintained, if hillier and more winding than travellers used to flat A-roads might expect.

## Getting to the Cíes Islands and the Pontevedra coast

The Cíes Islands, part of the Atlantic Islands of Galicia National Park, are only reachable by ferry, with sailings typically from Vigo's Estación Marítima in the city centre and, in summer, from Baiona and Cangas as well. Visitor numbers are capped daily, so it's worth booking ferry tickets in advance rather than turning up on spec. Having your own car makes it easy to base yourself further along the coast, in Sanxenxo or the fishing village of Combarro, and drive to whichever port has a sailing that suits your day, rather than being tied to one bus timetable.
