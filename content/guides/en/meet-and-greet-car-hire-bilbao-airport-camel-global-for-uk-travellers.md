## Driving in and Around Bilbao

UK drivers switching to the right-hand side of the road need a short adjustment period, and Bilbao's ring roads and tunnels are a reasonable place to do it steadily rather than in a rush. The city is hilly and compact, with several road tunnels cutting through to the Nervion estuary, and roundabout etiquette follows the standard continental give-way-to-the-left rule. The AP-8 motorway running along the northern coast is a toll road and the fastest way to cover distance towards San Sebastian or west towards Santander, while free alternatives like the N-634 are slower but scenic. Parking in the city centre is largely metered or in underground car parks; street parking near attractions like the Guggenheim is limited, so many visitors head straight for a signed car park (Iberdrola Tower or San Mames are common options) rather than circling for a space.

## From Arrivals to the Guggenheim Museum

The Guggenheim sits on the Abandoibarra waterfront, a straightforward drive of around 20 minutes from the airport along the A-8 and into the city via the tunnels. Titanium curves aside, the surrounding Ensanche district has several signposted car parks within a short walk of the museum entrance, making it a practical first stop before you check into a hotel or continue on to the coast.

## The Flysch Coast at Zumaia

For the layered rock formations at Zumaia, allow around 45 minutes from Bilbao, mostly along the AP-8 heading east towards San Sebastian before dropping down to the coast road. The car park near Itzurun beach fills up on sunny weekends, so an early start helps. Having your own car here matters: public transport along this stretch of coast is limited, and the flysch cliffs are best appreciated with the flexibility to stop at several viewpoints along the GI-3391.

## Rioja Alavesa Vineyards

Heading south instead, the vineyards of Rioja Alavesa are roughly an hour from Bilbao via the A-1 and A-3210, through increasingly dramatic hill country around the Sierra de Cantabria. Towns like Laguardia and Elciego, home to well-known wineries, are best explored by car since they're spread across a rural area with infrequent bus links. A hire car collected straight from the airport means you can be tasting Rioja by early afternoon on the day you land, rather than losing the morning to paperwork.
