## What Meet and Greet Car Hire Actually Means

Meet and greet car hire is simple in principle: rather than you going to the car, the car comes to you. A representative from the local hire company hands over the keys and paperwork at an agreed location, checks your documents, does a quick walk-round of the vehicle with you, and then you're free to drive off. When you return the car, the same process happens in reverse, often at the same spot or another convenient location you've agreed.

This is a genuine alternative to the traditional airport desk model, and it's particularly useful in a city like Malaga where distances between the terminal and rental compounds can add 20 to 30 minutes onto your journey before you've even started driving.

## Driving in Spain: What UK Visitors Should Know

The most obvious adjustment for UK drivers is that Spain drives on the right. This takes a little concentration for the first hour or so, particularly at roundabouts, junctions and when pulling out of car parks, but most drivers settle in quickly on the wider, well-signed roads around Malaga.

A few other practical points worth knowing before you set off:

- You must carry your full UK driving licence with you, and it's worth checking current requirements for an International Driving Permit well before you travel, as rules can change.
- Spanish law requires you to carry two warning triangles and a reflective vest in the car, and these should be provided by the hire company as part of the vehicle.
- Speed limits are posted in kilometres per hour, not miles, so keep an eye on your speedometer if it doesn't show both.
- On-the-spot fines can be issued for various offences, including speeding and unauthorised motorway use of certain lanes, so it pays to drive attentively rather than on autopilot.

## Roads Around Malaga and the Costa del Sol

The AP-7 and A-7 motorways run along the coast connecting Malaga to Marbella, Estepona and beyond towards Gibraltar, while the A-45 heads inland towards Antequera and eventually Granada. The AP-7 is a toll motorway in sections, while the A-7 running parallel is toll-free but can be busier, especially in July and August.

Malaga city centre itself has narrow one-way streets and limited parking, so many visitors find it easier to park on the outskirts or use a hotel car park rather than navigate the old town by car. If your plans include day trips to Ronda, Nerja or the White Villages, having a car delivered to your accommodation rather than collected from a city centre desk saves you the hassle of finding your way out of town before your holiday has properly started.
