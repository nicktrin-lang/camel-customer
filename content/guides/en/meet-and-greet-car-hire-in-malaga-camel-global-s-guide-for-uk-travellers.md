## Driving in Spain: What UK Visitors Need to Know

Spain drives on the right, which is the main adjustment for UK drivers used to the left. Give yourself a few minutes at the start of the journey to get comfortable, particularly at roundabouts and when pulling out of junctions, where instinct can pull you the wrong way. Speed limits are posted in kilometres per hour, not miles, and are generally 120 km/h on motorways (autopistas and autovias), 90 to 100 km/h on main roads, and 30 to 50 km/h in built-up areas. Tolls apply on some motorways around Malaga and the Costa del Sol, so it is worth checking your planned route in advance if you want to avoid them, since free alternative roads usually run alongside toll motorways.

UK driving licences are valid for driving in Spain, and you should carry your licence, passport, and booking confirmation with you. Spanish law also requires you to carry a reflective vest and a warning triangle in the car, and these are typically already provided by the local operator supplying your hire car.

## Roads Around Malaga and the Costa del Sol

The AP-7 and A-7 run along the coast connecting Malaga to Marbella, Fuengirola, and Estepona to the west, and Nerja and the Costa Tropical to the east. These roads are generally well maintained and easy for UK drivers to navigate, though summer traffic near the resort towns can be heavy, especially around Puerto Banus and Marbella town centre. If you are heading inland to Granada, Ronda, or Antequera, expect more winding roads through hills and mountains, where a smaller or mid-size car can be easier to handle than a large SUV. Parking in central Malaga and coastal towns is often metered on-street or in paid car parks, so factor that into your day if you are staying in a hotel without private parking.
