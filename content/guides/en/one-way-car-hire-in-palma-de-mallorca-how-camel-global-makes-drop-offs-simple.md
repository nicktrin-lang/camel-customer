## What Counts as One-Way Car Hire in Palma de Mallorca?

One-way hire simply means your pickup point and your drop-off point are different. In Mallorca this usually looks like one of the following:

- Fly into Palma de Mallorca Airport, collect a car, and leave it near your accommodation on the north or east coast (Alcúdia, Pollença, Cala Millor) at the end of your stay.
- Pick up at your hotel in Palma and drop off at the airport before your flight home, so you're not driving into departures traffic with luggage and a stressed schedule.
- Start in one resort area and finish in another if your holiday itinerary moves around the island.

Because Camel Global works on a meet & greet basis, the car is brought to wherever you need it and collected from wherever you finish, so a one-way route is simply a case of telling the operator both locations when you request quotes.

## What Should UK Drivers Know About Driving in Mallorca?

Mallorca drives on the right, so the biggest early adjustment for UK visitors is roundabouts (give way to the left, not the right) and remembering which side of the car to enter. A few practical points:

- Mallorca's main motorways, including the Ma-19 and Ma-20 ring road around Palma, are toll-free.
- Speed limits are generally 120 km/h on motorways, 90-100 km/h on main roads, and 50 km/h in towns unless signed otherwise.
- The Serra de Tramuntana road between Valldemossa, Deià and Sóller has tight hairpin bends and steep drops; drive at a gentle pace and use lower gears on the descents.
- A UK photocard driving licence is generally accepted for hire and driving in Spain; check your specific operator's requirements when you compare offers.
- Fuel stations are self-service and widely available, though rural stretches inland can have longer gaps between them, so don't leave it too late to fill up.

## What Are the Best Day Trips From Palma With a Hire Car?

Having your own car makes Mallorca's spread-out attractions genuinely reachable:

- **Valldemossa and Deià** for hillside villages, roughly 30-45 minutes from Palma.
- **Port de Sóller**, reached via the old mountain road or the tunnel, good for a coastal lunch.
- **Cap de Formentor**, a scenic drive to the lighthouse at the island's northern tip.
- **Alcúdia and its old town walls**, plus nearby beaches on the north coast.
- **Es Trenc**, a well-known stretch of sand on the south coast, around 40 minutes from Palma.
- **Coves del Drac** near Porto Cristo, the underground caves on the east side of the island.

A one-way hire suits this kind of touring holiday well if you're overnighting in more than one area rather than basing yourself in Palma the whole time.
