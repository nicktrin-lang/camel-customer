## Driving in and Around Malaga: What UK Visitors Should Know

Spain drives on the right, which takes a bit of adjusting for UK drivers used to the opposite side. Roundabouts, junctions, and overtaking all feel different for the first day or two, so give yourself a calm first drive, ideally somewhere less busy than the airport approach roads, to settle in.

A valid UK driving licence is generally accepted for tourists driving in Spain, and you should always carry it with you along with your passport when driving. It's worth checking current requirements before you travel, since rules can be updated.

Malaga's road network includes fast dual carriageways such as the AP-7 toll motorway along the coast and the free A-7, which runs parallel and is busier but avoids the toll. If you're heading towards Marbella or Estepona, the AP-7 is usually the smoother option, particularly in summer traffic. Speed limits are in kilometres per hour, not miles, and speed cameras are common on the main routes, so keep an eye on your speedo rather than relying on habit.

Parking in Malaga city centre can be tight, and many streets use blue or green zone paid parking during the day. Multi-storey car parks are widely available and often the simplest option if you're staying centrally. Away from the city, parking is generally easier, especially at coastal towns and inland villages.
