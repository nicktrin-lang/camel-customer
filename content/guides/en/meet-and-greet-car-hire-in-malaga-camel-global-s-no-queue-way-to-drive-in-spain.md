## Driving in Spain: What UK Visitors Should Know

Spain drives on the right, which takes a little adjustment for UK drivers used to the opposite side. Roundabouts, junctions, and dual carriageways all work in mirror image to what you're used to at home, so it's worth taking the first few miles slowly, particularly on unfamiliar roads around Malaga's airport approach.

Spanish motorways (autovías and autopistas) are generally well maintained. Some are toll roads, marked with a peaje sign, and tolls are usually paid at booths using cash or card. Speed limits are posted in kilometres per hour, not miles, so keep an eye on the signage rather than relying on habit.

UK licence holders can drive in Spain using a valid UK driving licence for tourism purposes, though it's sensible to carry your passport and licence together at all times, as Spanish police can request identification during routine checks. Parking in central areas of Malaga, Marbella, or Granada can be tightly regulated with blue and green zones, so check local signage before leaving a car on the street.
