# Car Hire Delivered to Bilbao Airport: Camel Global's Meet and Greet Service

## Why Queue When the Car Can Meet You at BIO?

Bilbao Airport (BIO), in Loiu, sits around 12km north of the city centre and handles a steady flow of UK arrivals, particularly from London, Manchester, Bristol and Edinburgh. It's a compact, single-terminal airport, so once you're through passport control and baggage reclaim, you're only a short walk from the arrivals hall doors. That short walk is exactly where meet and greet car hire earns its keep. Rather than following signs to an off-site rental compound or a shuttle bus, you arrange for a local operator to have the car ready and waiting near arrivals, keys in hand, paperwork done. There's no desk, no separate queue, no depot transfer to eat into your first afternoon in the Basque Country.

## From the Terminal to the Basque Coast: What the Drive Looks Like

Once you're in the car, Bilbao Airport connects quickly to the wider Basque road network. The BI-737 links the airport to the AP-8 motorway, which runs east towards San Sebastian and the French border, or west towards Santander and Asturias. If you're heading south, the AP-1 climbs towards Vitoria-Gasteiz and on to the Rioja wine region. San Sebastian is a comfortable hour's drive along the AP-8, and coastal towns like Getxo, Mundaka or Bermeo are reachable within thirty to forty-five minutes on smaller roads such as the BI-631 or N-637. Do bear in mind the AP-8 and AP-1 are toll motorways, so keep a card or some cash to hand at the peaje booths, or take the slower, free N-634 if you're not in a rush and fancy a more scenic coastal route.

## Driving and Parking in Bilbao City Centre

Bilbao itself has a low emission zone (Zona de Bajas Emisiones) covering much of the centre, and streets in areas like Casco Viejo are narrow, one-way and not always forgiving for larger vehicles. If your itinerary includes a night or two in the city before heading out to the coast, it's worth choosing a hotel with its own car park or using one of the well-signposted underground public car parks near the Guggenheim or Plaza Moyua, rather than hunting for street parking. Remember that in Spain you drive on the right, priority at roundabouts generally favours traffic already on the roundabout, and speed limits are posted in kilometres per hour, all of which take a few minutes to adjust to after driving in the UK.

## Practical Tips for UK Travellers

A full UK driving licence is valid for hire and driving in Spain, but bring the physical card, as some operators will want to see it alongside your passport. Spanish law requires drivers to carry a reflective jacket and warning triangles in the vehicle, and to keep spare glasses in the car if you wear them for driving, so check these are included when your car is delivered. If you're worried about a large excess being held against your credit card, ask the operator directly what applies to your booking before you travel, since terms vary between local suppliers. And if you're driving on to France via the AP-8, make sure your hire agreement permits cross-border travel.
