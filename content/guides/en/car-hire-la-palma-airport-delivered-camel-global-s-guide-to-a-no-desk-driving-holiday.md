## Why La Palma Deserves a Spot on Your Driving Itinerary

La Palma is often called "La Isla Bonita", the pretty island, and it earns the name through contrast rather than crowds. Within a single day's drive you can move from arid volcanic badlands to lush laurisilva forest dripping with moisture, then climb to an observatory-topped summit above the clouds. The road network is compact but winding, which makes a hire car far more useful than any bus timetable, especially if you want to reach trailheads, viewpoints, and villages that public transport simply doesn't serve well.

## Landing at La Palma Airport: What to Expect

La Palma Airport sits on the east coast, a short drive from Santa Cruz de la Palma, the island's capital. ## Driving in La Palma: What UK Visitors Should Know

Spain drives on the right, so if you're used to UK roads, give yourself a few calm minutes near the airport before tackling anything ambitious. La Palma's roads are generally well maintained but genuinely mountainous: expect frequent hairpin bends, sudden changes in altitude, and occasional low cloud or mist as you climb, particularly heading north towards the central ridge. Fuel stations are less frequent than on the bigger islands, so it's worth topping up before heading into rural or mountain areas. Speed limits are lower than they might appear necessary given the road quality, and that's usually because of blind bends rather than caution for its own sake. A smaller or mid-sized car is often more comfortable than a large vehicle on some of the narrower lanes around the north.

## Building Your Route: Ruta de los Volcanes in the South

The south of La Palma is defined by volcanic activity, most recently and visibly around the Cumbre Vieja area. The Ruta de los Volcanes is a well-known hiking route tracing a chain of volcanic craters, and while the walk itself is on foot, a car is what gets you to and from the various access points, such as El Pilar or Los Canarios (Fuencaliente), efficiently. Driving through this part of the island, you'll pass black lava fields, vineyards planted directly into volcanic ash, and views out towards the Atlantic that look almost lunar in places. This is a part of the island best explored slowly, with the flexibility to stop at viewpoints whenever the light is good.

## North to Los Tilos and the Laurel Forest

Head north and the landscape changes dramatically. Los Tilos, part of the Los Tilos Biosphere Reserve, is one of the best-preserved laurel forests in the Canary Islands, a cool, green, almost prehistoric contrast to the volcanic south. The drive there winds through deep ravines and past waterfalls, and having your own car means you can time your visit for quieter hours rather than fitting around a tour bus schedule. It's a slower drive than it looks on the map, so allow more time than you'd expect for the distance.

## Roque de los Muchachos: The Island's High Point

No driving holiday on La Palma is complete without the climb to Roque de los Muchachos, at around 2,400 metres the island's highest point and home to one of the world's most respected astronomical observatories. The road up is long, winding, and occasionally exposed to cloud, but the reward is a viewpoint above much of the weather, looking down into the vast Caldera de Taburiente.
