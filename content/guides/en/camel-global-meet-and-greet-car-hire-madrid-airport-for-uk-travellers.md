## Touching Down at Madrid-Barajas: Terminals and Arrivals

Madrid-Barajas is a large airport spread across four terminal buildings, T1, T2, T3 and T4, with T4 and its satellite T4S serving many long-haul and Star Alliance-style routes while T1 to T3 handle a mix of other carriers, including several UK low-cost routes. If you are flying in on a budget airline, there is a fair chance you will land at T1 or T4, both of which have their own separate arrivals halls and, importantly, their own rental car areas some distance apart. If you have pre-booked a traditional rental car and picked the wrong terminal assumption, you can lose 20 to 30 minutes just getting to the right counter.

Meet and greet car hire removes that terminal guesswork almost entirely. Instead of working out which building houses which hire company's desk, you agree a pickup point with the operator, typically the arrivals area of the terminal you are actually flying into, and they meet you there. This matters more at Barajas than at smaller regional airports precisely because the terminal layout is so spread out and the walk between buildings is not short.

## Driving in Madrid and Beyond: What UK Drivers Should Know

Spain drives on the right, which takes most UK drivers a short adjustment period, particularly at roundabouts and when pulling out of junctions. Madrid's ring roads, the M-30 and M-40, are the fastest way to skirt the city centre if you are heading straight out on a road trip rather than staying in town. Central Madrid itself operates Madrid Central, a low emission zone with restricted access for higher-emission vehicles, so if your route takes you into the historic centre, check your hire car's environmental badge or plan a route that uses the underground car parks instead of street parking.

Motorways in Spain are generally well maintained, and many of the fastest routes out of Madrid, towards Toledo, Segovia or Zaragoza, are toll roads (autopistas) alongside free alternatives (autovías), so it is worth knowing which route your sat nav is choosing. Fuel stations are frequent along all major routes, and diesel and petrol are both widely available, though it is always worth confirming with your chosen operator which fuel your specific vehicle takes before you set off.
