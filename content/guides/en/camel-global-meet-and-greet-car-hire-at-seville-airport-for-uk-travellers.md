## Getting Out of the Airport and Into the City

Seville Airport sits around 10 kilometres east of the city centre, just off the A-4 motorway. Once you're behind the wheel, the drive into the old town, Santa Cruz, the Cathedral, Triana across the river, typically takes 15 to 20 minutes outside peak traffic. The approach roads are well signposted, and the SE-30 ring road lets you skirt the centre if your accommodation is on the far side of the city rather than pushing straight through it. One thing worth knowing before you arrive: much of the historic centre, including large parts of Santa Cruz, is a restricted low-emission and pedestrian zone with narrow one-way streets. Most visitors park at the edge of the old town or at their hotel, if it has parking, rather than trying to drive directly to a central door.

## South to Doñana's Wetlands

One of the most rewarding day trips from Seville is south-west towards the Doñana National Park, one of Europe's most important wetland reserves and a stop-off point for migratory birds crossing from Africa. The drive from Seville to villages on the park's edge, such as El Rocío, takes roughly an hour along the A-49 and A-483, through flat, open countryside that looks nothing like the city you've just left. Roads here are quieter than around Seville itself, though sandy tracks near the park boundary can be slow going, so a car with reasonable ground clearance is worth requesting if you plan to explore beyond the main viewpoints. Having your hire car already with you from the moment you land makes an early start to Doñana far more realistic than adding a rental desk stop first.

## East to Carmona, a Short, Easy Drive

If you'd rather stay closer to Seville, Carmona is one of the simplest and most satisfying trips out of the city. It's barely half an hour east along the A-4, and the hilltop old town, with its Roman necropolis and Alcázar walls, rewards visitors without the crowds of central Seville. Parking near Carmona's old quarter is more straightforward than in Seville itself, with space just outside the walls, making it a good option for a relaxed half-day out with your own car rather than joining an organised tour.

## North into the Sierra Norte

For a different landscape altogether, the Sierra Norte de Sevilla natural park lies about an hour and a half north of the city along the A-66 and smaller regional roads. Expect winding, hilly routes through cork oak forest and whitewashed villages such as Cazalla de la Sierra and Constantina. These roads are narrower and less trafficked than the motorways around Seville, so a hire car with a bit more familiarity built in before you tackle them, gained on the easier drive out of the airport, makes the mountain stretches far less daunting.

## What UK Drivers Should Know Before Setting Off

Spain drives on the right, which takes most UK visitors a short adjustment period, particularly at roundabouts and when overtaking. Tolls exist on some motorways but the routes described here are largely toll-free. Fuel stations are plentiful along the A-4 and A-49 corridors, less so once you're deep in the Sierra Norte, so it's worth filling up before heading into the hills. With Camel Global, you pay a refundable fuel deposit alongside the hire cost, and the operator handles the return arrangement directly with you at handover, so it's worth asking your chosen local company how they'd like the car returned.
