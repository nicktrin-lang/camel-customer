## Driving in Spain: what UK visitors should know

Spain drives on the right, which takes a little adjustment for UK drivers, particularly at roundabouts and when overtaking. Motorways around Malaga, including the AP-7 and the A-7, are generally well maintained, and tolls apply on some stretches, notably parts of the AP-7 towards Marbella and further along the coast, so it is worth having a card or some change to hand. Speed limits are posted in kilometres per hour rather than miles, and Spanish traffic police can issue on the spot fines for common offences, so it pays to keep an eye on signage. A UK driving licence is generally accepted for short visits, but it is worth checking current requirements before you travel, since rules can be updated. Parking in central Malaga and in resort towns along the coast is often metered or restricted to residents, so many visitors find it easier to park at their accommodation and use the car mainly for trips further afield.

## Roads around Malaga and the Costa del Sol

The road network radiating from Malaga is generally straightforward for visitors. The coastal motorway makes towns like Torremolinos, Benalmadena, Fuengirola and Marbella easy to reach within an hour or so, while inland routes towards Antequera or up to Ronda involve more winding mountain roads that reward a bit of extra care, especially if you are not used to driving on the right. Signage is generally clear and bilingual in tourist areas, though smaller villages inland can have narrower streets than UK drivers might expect, so a compact car is often a practical choice if you plan to explore old town centres.
