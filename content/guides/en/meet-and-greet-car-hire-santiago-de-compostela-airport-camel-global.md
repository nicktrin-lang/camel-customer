## Reaching the Camino's Final Stretch

Many UK travellers land at SCQ specifically to join or finish the Camino de Santiago, and having a car delivered to arrivals is particularly useful if you're supporting walkers, carrying luggage between stages, or simply want to reach the cathedral city without messing about with buses. The airport sits around 10 kilometres north of Santiago's historic centre, a drive of roughly fifteen to twenty minutes on the AC-841 and AG-56. If your plan involves picking up walkers at Monte do Gozo, the traditional final viewpoint before pilgrims descend into the city, or dropping bags at accommodation inside the old town before parking outside the pedestrianised centre, having your own car from the moment you land makes the logistics far simpler than relying on taxis or infrequent local buses.

## Driving Galicia's Roads: What to Expect

Galicia's road network is generally excellent by rural Spanish standards, with dual carriageways connecting Santiago to A Coruña, Vigo and Lugo. Away from these main routes, expect narrower, winding roads through eucalyptus woodland and farmland, particularly if you're heading towards the coast. Speed limits are lower than UK motorway equivalents, tolls are limited on this stretch of Galicia's network, and fuel stations are frequent enough that you won't need to plan long detours. Driving is on the right, junctions and roundabouts follow standard Spanish priority rules, and UK licences are accepted for short-term hire and driving in Spain. Galician weather turns quickly, with mist and rain rolling in off the Atlantic even in summer, so it's worth checking conditions before setting out towards the coast.

## Costa da Morte and the Rías Altas by Car

A hire car really earns its keep once you leave Santiago itself. Costa da Morte, the dramatic Atlantic coastline west of the city, is scattered with lighthouses, granite cliffs and small ports like Muxía and Fisterra, none of which are well served by public transport. Further north, the Rías Altas offer a quieter run of fishing villages, estuaries and beaches around Ferrol and Cedeira. Both regions are built around slow, scenic driving rather than fast transfers, and having your own car delivered at the airport rather than collected from a depot means you can head straight out towards the coast the same day, rather than losing daylight hours to admin.
